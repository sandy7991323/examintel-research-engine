# Human Writing Quality Checklist

Run this checklist on every piece of generated content to ensure it reads like natural, human-written study material — not AI output.

---

## Banned Phrases Check

Scan the content for these AI-typical phrases. If found, **rewrite the sentence**:

- [ ] "It is important to note that" → Remove or replace with direct statement
- [ ] "In today's modern world" → Remove unless genuinely relevant
- [ ] "Delve into" / "delving into" → Use "look at", "explore", "examine"
- [ ] "Multifaceted" → Use "complex" or specific description
- [ ] "Leverage" → Use "use" or "take advantage of"
- [ ] "Utilize" → Use "use"
- [ ] "Paramount" → Use "very important" or "critical"
- [ ] "Aforementioned" → Name the thing directly
- [ ] "It should be noted" → State the fact directly
- [ ] "Furthermore" (overused) → Use "Also", "Another point", or restructure
- [ ] "In conclusion" (every answer) → Vary: "To summarize", "The key point is", or just state it
- [ ] "Comprehensive overview" → Use "complete look" or "full picture"
- [ ] "Plays a crucial role" → Say what it actually does
- [ ] "Stands as a testament to" → Remove
- [ ] "In the realm of" → Use "in" or name the field
- [ ] "Paves the way for" → Use "leads to" or "enables"
- [ ] "A myriad of" → Use "many" or a specific number
- [ ] "Serves as a cornerstone" → Explain why it matters instead
- [ ] "Facilitates the optimization of" → Say what it actually improves
- [ ] "Embark on a journey" → Remove

## Sentence Structure

- [ ] Sentences vary in length (mix of short and medium sentences)
- [ ] Average sentence length is under 25 words
- [ ] No paragraph contains 5+ sentences of identical length
- [ ] Active voice is used more than passive voice
- [ ] No sentence exceeds 40 words without good reason

## Tone and Voice

- [ ] Content reads like a teacher explaining to students
- [ ] Explanations tell WHY something works, not just WHAT it is
- [ ] Real-world examples are included where they help
- [ ] Technical terms are used correctly AND explained simply
- [ ] The tone is helpful and clear, not formal and distant
- [ ] Transitions between paragraphs feel natural

## Structure Variety

- [ ] Not every answer follows the exact same template
- [ ] Short answers are genuinely short (not padded)
- [ ] Long answers have genuine depth (not just more words)
- [ ] Bullet points are used for lists, paragraphs for explanations
- [ ] No answer starts by restating the question in different words
- [ ] Introduction sentences vary across answers

## Content Quality

- [ ] Every paragraph adds educational value
- [ ] No empty filler paragraphs ("In this section, we will discuss...")
- [ ] Examples are specific and concrete (not vague)
- [ ] Comparisons highlight genuine differences (not trivial ones)
- [ ] Definitions use the student's vocabulary, then introduce technical terms
- [ ] Grammar is correct throughout
- [ ] Factual information is accurate

## Good vs. Bad Examples

### BAD (AI-like):
> "In today's rapidly evolving technological landscape, data structures play a paramount role in facilitating the efficient organization and manipulation of data. It is important to note that understanding these fundamental concepts is crucial for any aspiring computer science professional."

### GOOD (Human-like):
> "Data structures are ways to organize and store data so that programs can access and modify it quickly. Learning them well makes you a better programmer because almost every piece of software depends on choosing the right data structure for the job."

### BAD:
> "A stack is a linear data structure that serves as a cornerstone in the realm of computer science. It leverages the Last-In-First-Out principle to facilitate data management."

### GOOD:
> "A stack is a simple data structure that works like a pile of plates. The last plate you put on top is the first one you pick up. This is called Last-In-First-Out (LIFO)."

---

## Completion Criteria

Writing quality passes when:
1. Zero banned phrases remain in the content
2. Sentence length varies naturally
3. Active voice predominates
4. At least one real example per major topic
5. Content reads like prepared classroom notes, not AI output
